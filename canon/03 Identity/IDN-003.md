# 1. Purpose

The Identity Architecture defines the canonical architectural organization of the Identity domain within Kaivo Core.

It establishes the conceptual structure, responsibilities, and relationships of Identity artifacts while preserving canonical consistency, traceability, and implementation neutrality.

---

# 2. Scope

The Identity Architecture defines:

* Identity architectural organization.
* Identity architectural responsibilities.
* Identity architectural relationships.
* Identity architectural boundaries.
* Identity conformance requirements.

The Identity Architecture does not define:

* Authentication.
* Authorization.
* Runtime behavior.
* Technical implementation.
* Infrastructure architecture.

---

# 3. Architectural Principles

The Identity Architecture shall be:

* Canonical.
* Modular.
* Consistent.
* Traceable.
* Reusable.
* Stable.
* Implementation-neutral.

---

# 4. Architectural Organization

The Identity domain is organized as a coherent collection of canonical artifacts that collectively define, specify, register, map, and standardize Identity.

Each Identity artifact fulfills one distinct architectural responsibility.

The Identity Architecture preserves these responsibilities without overlap or duplication.

---

# 5. Architectural Relationships

Identity artifacts shall:

* Conform to the Core Ontology.
* Conform to the Core Asset Model.
* Conform to the Identity Vision.
* Conform to the Identity Principles.
* Preserve canonical terminology.
* Preserve approved dependency relationships.

Identity artifacts shall not redefine the responsibilities of other canonical artifacts.

---

# 6. Architectural Responsibilities

The Identity Architecture shall:

* Organize Identity artifacts into a coherent architectural collection.
* Preserve the integrity of Identity definitions.
* Maintain architectural consistency.
* Preserve traceability across the Identity domain.

---

# 7. Boundaries

The Identity Architecture does not define:

* Security architecture.
* Runtime execution.
* Infrastructure.
* Repository implementation.
* Application-specific behavior.

---

# 8. Canonical Constraints

### C1

Every Identity artifact shall have exactly one architectural responsibility.

---

### C2

Identity responsibilities shall not overlap.

---

### C3

Identity artifacts shall preserve approved canonical terminology.

---

### C4

Identity artifacts shall preserve approved dependency relationships.

---

### C5

The Identity Architecture shall conform to the Core Ontology.

---

### C6

The Identity Architecture shall conform to the Core Asset Model.

---

### C7

The Identity Architecture shall remain implementation-neutral.

---

# 9. Conformance

An Identity artifact conforms to the Identity Architecture if it:

* Preserves its defined architectural responsibility.
* Maintains architectural consistency.
* Preserves approved dependency relationships.
* Preserves canonical terminology.
* Conforms to the Core Ontology.
* Conforms to the Core Asset Model.
* Conforms to the Identity Vision and Identity Principles.
