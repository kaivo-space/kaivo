# 1. Purpose

The Identity Model defines the canonical conceptual structure of Identity within Kaivo Core.

It establishes the shared model used to represent uniquely identifiable entities while remaining independent of implementation, storage technologies, programming languages, and application domains.

The Identity Model serves as the conceptual foundation for all identity-related specifications, templates, registries, and maps.

---

# 2. Scope

The Identity Model defines:

* The concept of Identity
* Identity composition
* Identity characteristics
* Identity relationships
* Identity lifecycle principles
* Canonical identity constraints

The Identity Model does not define:

* Authentication
* Authorization
* Access control
* Security policies
* User accounts
* Database schemas
* Runtime behavior
* User interfaces

---

# 3. Identity Definition

An Identity is the canonical representation of a uniquely identifiable entity within Kaivo.

Identity provides persistent recognition across time, independent of changes to descriptive information, implementation details, or system environments.

Identity represents continuity rather than mutable state.

---

# 4. Design Principles

The Identity Model follows these principles.

## Uniqueness

Every Identity represents one and only one identifiable entity.

---

## Stability

Identity remains stable throughout the lifecycle of the represented entity.

---

## Persistence

Identity persists independently of changes to descriptive information.

---

## Reusability

Identity may be referenced by multiple Core Assets without duplication.

---

## Traceability

Identity remains uniquely referenceable throughout its lifecycle.

---

## Independence

Identity is independent of implementation technologies and storage mechanisms.

---

# 5. Identity Composition

Every Identity consists of two conceptual layers.

## Core Identity

The immutable conceptual identity of the represented entity.

This layer provides continuity and uniqueness.

---

## Identity Description

The descriptive information associated with the Identity.

Descriptions may evolve without changing the Identity itself.

---

# 6. Identity Characteristics

Every Identity is:

* Unique
* Stable
* Persistent
* Reusable
* Traceable
* Referenceable
* Version-independent
* Implementation-neutral

---

# 7. Identity Lifecycle

Identity exists continuously throughout the lifecycle of the represented entity.

Lifecycle events may modify descriptive information but shall not replace the Identity unless the represented entity itself changes.

The lifecycle model is defined by higher-level Core artifacts.

---

# 8. Identity Relationships

The Identity Model establishes the following canonical relationships.

## Identity ↔ Core Asset

Every Identity is represented as a Core Asset.

---

## Identity ↔ Core Asset Model

The structural principles governing Identity are inherited from the Core Asset Model.

---

## Identity ↔ Identity Specification

The Identity Specification defines the formal structure and validation rules for Identity instances.

---

## Identity ↔ Templates

Templates provide reusable structures for creating Identity instances that conform to the Identity Specification.

---

## Identity ↔ Registry

The Identity Registry maintains authoritative records of Identity instances.

---

## Identity ↔ Identity Map

The Identity Map represents relationships among identities without modifying their semantics.

---

# 9. Boundaries

The Identity Model does not define:

* Organizational structures
* Human resource models
* Security architecture
* Permission systems
* Business workflows
* Runtime execution
* User interface behavior

These concerns belong to higher-level Core artifacts.

---

# 10. Canonical Constraints

The following constraints are normative.

### C1

An Identity shall represent exactly one identifiable entity.

---

### C2

An Identity shall possess a stable conceptual identity throughout its lifecycle.

---

### C3

Descriptive information shall not redefine Identity.

---

### C4

Identity shall remain uniquely referenceable.

---

### C5

Identity shall be reusable across Core Assets.

---

### C6

Identity shall remain implementation-neutral.

---

### C7

Identity shall conform to the Core Ontology.

---

### C8

Identity shall conform to the Core Asset Model.

---

# 11. Conformance

An implementation conforms to the Identity Model if it:

* Represents Identity according to this model.
* Preserves stable conceptual identity.
* Separates Identity from descriptive information.
* Maintains unique referenceability.
* Conforms to the Core Ontology.
* Conforms to the Core Asset Model.
